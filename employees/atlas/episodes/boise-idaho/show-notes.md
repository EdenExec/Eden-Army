## Three takeaways (as spoken)

1. Boise is a desert made green by water, and water is still its biggest long-term question.
2. Construction is the engine: metro construction jobs more than doubled from 2015 to 2025, a $50 billion chip build is underway, and skilled people are the scarce thing.
3. Blue city, red state: the statehouse usually has the last word, so learn both.

**Question for the road:** ten years from now, when people in the valley talk about your family, what will they say you built there, and who did you build it for?

## How the script handles the hard and uncertain claims

**Conflicts, told in one line or settled conservatively**
- Capital date: the historical society's 24 Dec 1864 and 14 Apr 1865, with one line noting that reference books say 1866. The "one-vote court margin" is left out.
- Boise population: "between 240,000 and 255,000, depending on who's counting", with the Census and COMPASS gap named.
- Micron: $15 billion announced in 2022, now "described as about $50 billion" for two fabs; first wafers mid-2027 with one outlet's "second half of 2027"; CHIPS support "a little over six billion, varies by source". The 35% tax credit (†) and the conflicting Micron headcounts are left out.
- Arrowrock: "for years the tallest dam in the world" (sources say 9 or about 20 years).

- Unconfirmed reporting: the housing price history before Oct 2025, the pride-flag bill (said "moved", not "signed"), the private-school tax credit and its court case, the Hoffman/Exyte change, the New York delay, the reservoirs carrying 2026, the 2055 forecast, the growth survey.
