## Verdict: SIGN WITH FIXES (do not send as is)

It reads plainly and the deal is right. Six short fixes, mostly one sentence each, stop two agreements from both applying, close a circular payment date, and keep Eden's leverage on the $37,000 past due.

## Top three issues

1. **Two agreements stay live (s.6, no whole-agreement clause).** The June agreement runs 365 days from signing (to about June 2027) at 25%. Nothing in the new Doc says it replaces June for new candidates, and s.6 lets "Eden's senior leadership team" move candidates between terms on its own say-so. Every fee becomes arguable: at a $200,000 base that is $50,000 versus $36,000.
2. **Retainer due date is circular (3.1, 4.1, 4.2).** 3.1 says due "by the 15th"; 4.1 says Eden invoices "on its due date"; 4.2 says pay "within 15 days" of the invoice. Read together, the retainer is due the 30th, not the 15th.
3. **The tail contradicts itself and misses contract hires (3.5, 7.3).** 3.5 counts 12 months from presentation; 7.3 counts 12 months from the end of the Agreement. And "accepts employment" does not catch a contract hire, while the fee base ("annual base salary") does not exist for one.

## Law notes (Washington, assumed from 8.3)

- **The 12-month tail and contract hires are a fee, not a restraint: LIKELY enforceable.** RCW 49.62.010(4) (version in force until 30 Jun 2027; read 25 Nov 2026) reaches covenants that bar an employee or contractor from work. Clause 3.5 binds VMG to pay, not the candidate to stay away. The 2026 amendment (c 149, from 30 Jun 2027) adds forfeiture terms, still aimed at the worker. June already covered "employee, contractor, or consultant" for one year, so this is continuity.
- **"Any company VMG controls" binds VMG, not the affiliate: LIKELY.** The risk is detection, not enforceability. It is narrower than June's "affiliates", which favours VMG.
- **No-hire of Eden staff (option D): UNVERIFIED this session.** Washington's AG has pressed no-poach clauses between businesses under the Consumer Protection Act; a fee on hiring, rather than a ban, is the safer form. Check with counsel before using a ban.
