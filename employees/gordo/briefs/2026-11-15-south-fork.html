South Fork Trip Brief

Go to the South Fork Boise below Anderson Ranch Dam, if you can be out of town by noon. If not, fish the Boise River in town.

1. Where to go

Pick: South Fork below the dam. Flow is low and flat, which is as good as it gets for a wading angler: 285 to 314 cfs for seven days, so no release surprise in the data. The roaded stretch has a dirt road that follows the river for roughly 10 miles to Danskin Bridge, so you can pick water you can walk to. LIKELY

Backup: Boise River in town (gauge at Glenwood Bridge reads 249 cfs, steady-ish, 60F and partly sunny, southeast wind 8 to 15). Ten to twenty minutes from home, same flies, no canyon road. Rain moves in Sunday night. Fish the Greenbelt or Barber Park water. LIKELY

Pick the backup if

  • You are not out of town by noon. A late start gives you about an hour of fishing after the drive and a dark canyon road home.
  • The USGS dam number moves more than about 50 cfs from 304 by morning, or Idaho 511 shows a closure on US-20 or the dam road.
  • You would rather get three hours of fishing than three hours of driving. Honest call: the town river is the better practice day. The South Fork is the better fish day.

2. Getting there

  1. I-84 east from west Boise to Mountain Home, about 40 miles.
  2. US-20 north from Mountain Home about 20 miles to Anderson Ranch Dam Road.
  3. Cross the dam, head downstream. Forest Road 113 follows the river about 10 miles to Danskin Bridge.

Time: about 1 hour 45 minutes to the dam, about 2 hours to the lower pull-outs. That is my estimate from published directions, not a routing app (A). Put the dam road in Google Maps tonight and download the map offline.

Car: the Tundra. The river road is gravel and dirt, shaded sections can hold frost or mud in the morning, and Tundra clearance and tires are the safe pick. The LX 470 can do it; it is your second choice.

Road conditions: I could not read them. Idaho 511 is a live map and showed no announcement and no data I could load. Open it before you go: 511.idaho.gov, turn on Road Conditions and Active Closures for US-20 and I-84. UNSURE

3. Where to get in the water

  • Easiest for 4/10: the first pull-outs below the dam where the road runs right beside the river. Look for a wide riffle that slows into a run, a gravel bottom, a bank you can walk, and water you can reach without going past your knee. You can see what you are doing and bail in ten steps.
  • Second option: Cow Creek, about seven miles below the dam by one local guide, with a boat ramp and a road back to US-20 (a way out). LIKELY
  • Skip: below Danskin Bridge. No road, Class III to IV whitewater, a log jam near Neal Bridge. Not a wading trip.

I could not confirm named pull-outs or their parking from a primary source, so treat the spots above as a description to look for, not a map. Ask at the shop: "Where do you send a beginner on the South Fork this week?" Weekend crowds on this stretch are common, so give people room.

7. Rig and plan for the 9 ft 5 wt

Indicator nymph rig, step by step

  1. Tie a 9 ft 5X leader to your fly line. Add 2 ft of 5X tippet with a surgeon's knot so it is a bit longer.
  2. Slide an indicator on, set about 1.5 times the water depth above the top fly. Start at 5 feet. Peg it or use a twist-on.
  3. Tie your top fly (Hare's Ear 14 or Perdigon 16) to the end.
  4. Add a dropper: 14 to 18 inches of 5X tied to the top fly's hook bend, then a zebra midge or Pheasant Tail 18. (Fish one fly if you want to skip the two-fly question in section 5.)
  5. Pinch on one small split shot or tungsten putty, 8 to 10 inches above the top fly. Add one more if you are not ticking bottom.
  6. Cast upstream-ish, mend to keep the indicator moving at the speed of the water, and set the hook on any dip, stop or hesitation.

When to switch to dries

When you see noses or dimples on the surface, usually 1 to 3 PM, and bugs on the water. Pull the indicator and shot, lengthen to 9 ft of 5X plus 2 to 3 ft of 6X, tie on a Parachute Adams or BWO 18 to 20. Fish upstream of the rise and let it drift with no drag. No rises, stay on nymphs.

8. Pack list and safety

  • Wear: layers under the waders (fleece, not cotton), warm hat, gloves you can fish in, polarized glasses, and a blaze orange cap or vest. November hunting seasons are open and hunters are in the canyon.
  • Carry: wading belt cinched, 9 ft 5X leaders, 4X/5X/6X tippet, nippers, forceps, floatant, indicators, split shot, net, headlamp, whistle, water, food, charged phone, spare dry clothes in the truck, a full gas tank.
  • Cold: water is 51F. Air starts near 31F. Know the signs of hypothermia (shivering you can't stop, clumsy hands); if it starts, get out and warm up. Anything medical goes to Hale.
  • Dam release: the flow can change. Check the gauge when you arrive and again at 3. A rising water line, a sudden color change or a rising hum means get out now.
  • Wading: never above the knee in fast water. Shuffle, don't step. Face upstream, use the belt. If you go in, feet downstream, on your back, swim to the bank at an angle.
  • Cell coverage: I could not confirm it. Assume little to none in the canyon. UNSURE Download the offline map before you leave town.
  • Tell someone: Ally gets the water, the pull-out you picked, your leave time and "if you haven't heard by 6:30, call." You send it; I don't message anyone. Draft: "South Fork Boise below Anderson Ranch Dam, fishing from about 2 to 4:30, home by 6:30. Phone may not work in the canyon. Call if I'm not back by 7."

9. One skill for tomorrow

Dead drift with a clean mend. Your indicator should move at exactly the speed of the water. If it is skating or dragging, mend upstream and recast. Count: aim for ten good drifts per run, and set the hook on every hesitation, even the false ones.

You'll know it worked when you can tell a bottom tick from a take, and you have set the hook on at least three twitches and one of them was a fish. That is what moves a 4 toward a 5.
