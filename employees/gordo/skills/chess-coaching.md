# Chess coaching (for Kev himself)

Kev plays with G sometimes, and Atlas teaches G chess (`employees/atlas/episodes/chess/show-sheet.md`: the moves, checkmate versus stalemate, "what does my opponent want?", the f7 trap, the king and queen against king drill). Gordo coaches Kev's own game so he plays better, and plays G well: a strong dad who teaches, not one who flattens.

## Baseline (first chess session)

1. Does Kev have an online rating (Lichess, Chess.com) or a US Chess rating? If not: play ten rated rapid games (15 minutes plus a 10-second increment) on Lichess, which is free and ad-free (https://lichess.org). That rating is the baseline. Chess.com has free play too; any paid membership is a QC item and is not needed to start.
2. How much time per week: Gordo's default is 20 minutes a day on weekdays (A), plus one longer session.
3. What he wants: beat G more cleverly, play club games, or just think more clearly.

Record the rating and date in `memory/live/README.md`. Re-measure every four weeks.

## Openings to learn (keep it to three)

| Colour | Play | Why |
|---|---|---|
| White | 1.e4 and the Italian Game (1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4) | Fast development, the centre, early castling: the same principles Atlas is teaching G |
| Black against 1.e4 | 1...e5, meeting the Italian and the Ruy Lopez with simple development | Mirrors what he plays as White |
| Black against 1.d4 | The Queen's Gambit Declined (1.d4 d5 2.c4 e6) | Solid, few traps to memorise |

Learn the ideas (where the pieces go and why), not 20 moves deep. Ten moves of each, then play.

## Tactics routine (daily, 15 minutes)

- Lichess puzzles (free), themed: forks, pins, skewers, discovered attacks, back-rank mates.
- Before each move in a puzzle: checks, captures, threats, for both sides.
- Accuracy over speed: a wrong guess is a miss; write down the pattern you missed.

## Game review (after every rated game, 10 minutes)

1. Without the engine: where did the game turn? What was the plan?
2. Then the engine (free analysis on Lichess): find the biggest single mistake, not all of them.
3. Write one line in the log: the position type and the lesson ("left the back rank open", "traded the good bishop").
4. Once a week, look at the log: the pattern that repeats is next week's study.

## Weekly plan

| Day | 20 minutes |
|---|---|
| Mon, Tue, Wed, Thu | Tactics 15 + review yesterday's game 5 |
| One evening | A rated rapid game and its review |
| Sunday (optional) | A game with G, then one idea from it for Atlas |

Never the Sabbath: Gordo pushes nothing then. A game with G on the Sabbath is family, not training, and is never logged as practice.

## Playing G

- Ask Atlas first what G is on now (Radar or Atlas's Desk ask card), so the game practises it: development, guarding f7, the K+Q mate.
- Give odds that keep it close (play without a knight, or a time edge for G), and talk through one move each game: "What does my opponent want?"
- After the game, one line to Atlas: what G saw, what he missed, what he asked. No scores of a ten-year-old on any shared surface.
