## Rules that trip people up (upland booklet pp. 13, 25 and 43; waterfowl booklet p. 24)

- Upland birds: shotgun with shells no longer than 3.5 inches, or a muzzleloading shotgun (forest grouse: any firearm). No electronic calls, no bait.
- **Never shoot from or across a public road**, its shoulders or its embankments. No hunting from a motor vehicle.
- Shooting hours: half an hour before sunrise to half an hour after sunset; on pheasant release areas, 10:00am to half an hour after sunset during the pheasant season.
- Hunter orange, at least 36 square inches above the waist, on pheasant release areas during the pheasant season.
- Leave one fully feathered wing or the head attached until the bird is home or at the processor.
- Permission for private land, always; cultivated or posted land without it is trespass.
- Waterfowl: plugged gun (3 shells), nontoxic shot, HIP plus a signed duck stamp; the most common violations IDFG writes are an unsigned stamp and a missing HIP.

## Shotgun basics and safety

- The four rules every time: treat every gun as loaded; muzzle in a safe direction; finger off the trigger until ready to shoot; know your target and what is beyond it. In the field add: safe zones of fire with every partner (never swing through a partner or a dog), action open when crossing a fence or a ditch, unloaded in the truck, ear and eye protection.
- A 12- or 20-gauge pump or over-and-under covers every bird above. Fit matters more than brand: a gun that fits shoots where you look. Have a range or a shop check gun fit before buying anything; a purchase is a QC item.
- Shot (A, confirm with a shop): 7.5 or 6 for quail and chukar early; 5 or 6 for pheasants; nontoxic 3 or 2 steel for ducks, BB or larger for geese.
- Anything about firearm law (transport, carry while hunting, buying) is in `skills/idaho-gun-law.md`.
