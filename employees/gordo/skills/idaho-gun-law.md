# Idaho gun law (education only)

Kev, 15 Nov 2026: "Idaho gun laws in general." This playbook teaches the shape of the law so Kev knows what to ask and stays well inside the lines. **It is not legal advice.** Statutes change every session of the Legislature, and Idaho Code 18-3302 itself has a version that takes effect on 1 January 2027.

## How every answer is built

1. **Safety first, always.** Before any law: the four rules (every gun is loaded; muzzle safe; finger off the trigger; know the target and beyond), locked storage, unloaded in the truck.
2. **Cite and grade every claim.** Idaho Code (https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/), the Idaho State Police, IDFG or the ATF (https://www.atf.gov), or the other state's code. SETTLED means Gordo read the statute or agency page this session; LIKELY means a strong secondary source or an agency page that hedges; UNSURE means thin or conflicting sources, or the primary source was not opened.
3. **Close every answer with "check the current statute before you rely on it"**, and the link.
4. **Route real exposure in the first line.** A specific transfer, an inherited or gifted gun, carrying in Washington or Oregon, a self-defence scenario, anything after a police contact, a question about Kev's own eligibility: "This one is for a lawyer." Send the question (no family detail) to Docket as a review request through Radar; Docket says when a licensed Idaho attorney is needed (or a Washington or Oregon attorney for those states). Gordo never contacts a lawyer itself.
5. **Never help anyone evade the law:** no straw purchases, no workarounds for a prohibited place, no carrying where it is barred, no transfers that skip a required background check, no advice on hiding a firearm from police.

## Carry in Idaho (Idaho Code 18-3302, read 15 Nov 2026)

| Claim | Grade | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| A concealed-weapons license is not needed for a U.S. citizen (or current armed forces member) 18 or older who is not disqualified under 18-3302(11)(b) to (n): permitless concealed carry inside and outside city limits | SETTLED | 18-3302(4)(f) |
| Also exempt from the license requirement: open carry (a weapon in plain view), lawfully possessed rifles and shotguns, a weapon concealed in a motor vehicle, an unloaded firearm secured in a case, and carry at home, at your fixed place of business or on property you own or lease | SETTLED | 18-3302(3) and (4)(a) to (e) |
| Disqualifiers include a pending or past felony-level charge or conviction, unlawful drug use (including marijuana, which stays federally controlled), certain protection orders, certain mental-health findings, and anything that bars possession under Idaho or federal law | SETTLED | 18-3302(11) |
| Private property owners, tenants, employers and businesses keep the right to bar weapons; government property normally open to the public cannot restrict carry except for invitation-only, admission-charging or other restricted-access events | SETTLED | 18-3302(25) |
| Idaho issues a standard license and an **enhanced license** (with training); the enhanced license is the one other states (including Washington) recognise. Who issues it, the training and whether a new resident qualifies: ask the county sheriff (Ada County) | LIKELY (18-3302(20) points to 18-3302K, not opened) | 18-3302K; Idaho State Police and county sheriff pages |

## Where carry is prohibited

| Place | Rule | Grade | Cite |
|---|---|---|---|
| Courthouses, juvenile detention facilities and jails | No concealed carry, even with a license or under permitless carry, unless the authority over the building allows it | SETTLED | Idaho Code 18-3302C |
| Public or private school property, school-sponsored activities and school transportation | Possessing a firearm is a misdemeanor. Exceptions include an adult (not a student) whose firearm is "secured and locked in his vehicle in an unobtrusive, nonthreatening manner", and a person delivering or picking up children or students in a private vehicle | SETTLED | Idaho Code 18-3302D(4)(e) and (f) |
| Within 1,000 feet of a school (federal) | The federal Gun-Free School Zones Act exempts people licensed by that state and an unloaded firearm in a locked container; permitless carry alone may not meet the exemption. Practical rule: on school runs, unloaded and locked, or leave it home | LIKELY (statute not opened this session) | 18 U.S.C. 922(q)(2)(B) |
| Public colleges and universities | Restricted; enhanced license holders have a carve-out with exceptions | UNSURE (not opened) | Idaho Code 18-3309 |
| Federal facilities (federal courthouses, federal offices) | Prohibited | LIKELY | 18 U.S.C. 930 |
| Post office buildings and their property, including parking lots | Prohibited | LIKELY | 39 CFR 232.1(l) |
| Private businesses that post a ban; airports past security; tribal land; national park buildings | Their rules or federal rules apply | LIKELY | Each owner's or agency's rule |

## Vehicles

- In Idaho a weapon concealed in a motor vehicle is exempt from the license requirement [SETTLED, 18-3302(4)(c)]. Gordo's practice rule for hunting and fishing trips anyway: long guns unloaded in a case in the truck, every time.
- **Driving through other states:** federal law protects transport between two places where you may lawfully have the gun if it is unloaded and neither the gun nor ammunition is readily accessible from the passenger compartment (or both are in a locked container other than the glove box) [LIKELY, 18 U.S.C. 926A]. It does not cover stopping to carry, hunt or stay.

## Hunting-specific firearm rules (IDFG, read 15 Nov 2026, SETTLED)

- No shooting from or across a public road, its shoulders or its embankments; no hunting from a motor vehicle (upland booklet p. 43).
- Upland birds: shotgun shells no longer than 3.5 inches or a muzzleloading shotgun; forest grouse may be taken with any firearm (p. 13). Waterfowl: plugged to three shells, nontoxic shot (migratory booklet).
- Hunter orange (36 square inches above the waist) on pheasant release areas in the pheasant season (p. 12).
- A hunter carrying a handgun for defence while bird hunting is governed by the carry rules above; on federal land (BLM, Forest Service) state carry law generally applies outside federal buildings [LIKELY].

## Buying and transferring

| Claim | Grade | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| From a dealer (FFL): ATF Form 4473 and a NICS background check | SETTLED (federal baseline) | 18 U.S.C. 922(t); ATF |
| For the ATF, your state of residence is the state where you are present with the intention of making a home; someone with homes in two states is a resident of the one he is living in at the time. So Kev is likely an Idaho resident **for firearm purchases** now, even though IDFG's six-month rule still applies to licenses. His own case, with two homes, is a Docket question before he relies on it | LIKELY | 27 CFR 478.11; ATF |
| Handguns: an out-of-state purchase must go through a dealer in the buyer's state of residence. Long guns can be bought from a dealer in another state if the sale is lawful in both states | LIKELY | 18 U.S.C. 922(a)(3), (a)(5), (b)(3) |
| Idaho does not require a background check on a private sale between Idaho residents. Never sell or give a gun to anyone you know or suspect is prohibited | LIKELY | Secondary sources (Giffords Law Center); 18 U.S.C. 922(d) |
| No private transfer across state lines without a dealer, except some inheritances (lawyer question) | LIKELY | 18 U.S.C. 922(a)(5) |
| Selling or giving a firearm to a minor under 18 needs a parent's or guardian's written consent | LIKELY (statute named, text not opened) | Idaho Code 18-3308 |

## Storage around children

- **Idaho has no general child-access-prevention or safe-storage law** [LIKELY, Giffords Law Center summaries; check before relying]. Washington does (see below). The law is the floor, not the standard.
- Gordo's house rule, safety first: every gun not on a person is locked (a safe or a locked case with a cable lock), unloaded, ammunition stored separately and locked; keys and combinations known only to the adults who need them; G is taught "Stop, don't touch, leave the room, tell an adult" (Atlas can teach it; hand Atlas a topic brief).
- A minor under 18 may possess a weapon only with a parent's written permission or the parent's company; under 12, an adult must be with him [SETTLED, Idaho Code 18-3302E].
- Never record where the safe is, its combination, the inventory or serial numbers anywhere in the repo or on any shared surface.

## Washington: the Vancouver house (the law differs sharply)

| Claim | Grade | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| A concealed pistol license is required to carry a pistol concealed outside your home or fixed place of business | SETTLED | RCW 9.41.050(1)(a), https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=9.41.050 |
| No **loaded** pistol in a vehicle without a license. An **unloaded** pistol may be left in a vehicle only if it is locked inside and concealed from view | SETTLED | RCW 9.41.050(2)(a), (3)(a) |
| Washington honours **only the Idaho enhanced permit**, not the standard Idaho license; Idaho's permitless carry gives nothing in Washington | SETTLED (AG page updated 10 Jul 2026) | https://www.atg.wa.gov/concealed-weapon-reciprocity; RCW 9.41.073 |
| A permit to purchase firearms (state patrol, safety training, fingerprints) takes effect 1 May 2027, if funded | LIKELY | HB 1163 (2025), chapter 370, Laws of 2025 |
| Washington restricts the manufacture, import and sale of many semi-automatic rifles it calls assault weapons (2023) and of magazines over 10 rounds (2022); bringing either into Washington may count as import | UNSURE (not opened) | RCW 9.41.390, 9.41.370; lawyer question |
| Washington makes leaving a firearm where a prohibited person or a child can get it a crime when it is used or displayed | LIKELY (not opened) | RCW 9.41.360 |
| Washington has a waiting period and a training requirement on firearm purchases | UNSURE (not opened) | RCW 9.41.092 and related; check |

Gordo's rule for the Vancouver house: treat Washington as its own regime. Before carrying, transporting or storing anything there, open the RCW sections above, and send anything specific to Docket.

## Oregon: the Owyhee and the drive through

| Claim | Grade | Cite |
|---|---|---|

| Measure 114 (permit to purchase, magazine limits) is delayed by the Legislature and in the Oregon Supreme Court; one 2026 bill moves the permit to transfers from 1 January 2028, and sources disagree on the magazine date | UNSURE | OPB, 25 Feb 2026; Oregon Legislature HB 4145 (2026) analysis |

Gordo's rule for an Owyhee fishing trip: leave the handgun at home, or transport it unloaded and locked out of reach under 18 U.S.C. 926A, after reading the current ORS sections.
