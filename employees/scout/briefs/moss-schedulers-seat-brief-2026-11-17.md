## The seat
- A scheduler, not a manager. Two openings (West Palm, Miami). Team is 7: two planner/schedule managers, two senior schedulers, two schedulers, and Amut. Tight-knit, cross-trains, consults constantly.
- Goal: build the future scheduling lead, with a career path to independence. A scheduler can grow to senior scheduler or planner in 5 to 7 years.

## Must-dos
1. P6.
2. 2 to 5 years as a planner or scheduler. No more than 10 years. A strong 5 to 7 year person is fine.
3. Execution-side scheduling: understands how the job gets built and analytics. Not a heavy project-controls background (Moss has no project controls department).
4. In person only: no remote, no hybrid. Own desk in the office, 2 to 5 jobs each, site visits and time with the supers. Downtime goes to proposals. Flexible on office days if someone is near a site (for example Boca).
5. Commercial environment. High-rise is nice but does not matter (Amut had none before Moss). Mixed portfolio: roughly $20M school, $250M condo, $80M apartment.
6. Good profile sources: project engineers, assistant supers who want scheduling full time, supers converted to schedulers, someone who has gone in to fix a schedule. Many Moss people learned scheduling at Coastal or similar.
7. Willing to relocate is fine, but do not waste time.

## Personality match
Team player, grows in their own lane, no scarcity mindset, no cockiness or know-it-all. Likes to have fun, wants to be trained, wants to become an excellent scheduler. Everyone can become a leader here.

## Process notes
- First 3 months: shadow team members, meet project teams, learn the Moss way. No output expected. Then their own work, plus a passion area (for example Formula 1 or sports projects).
- West Palm hires stay within one county where possible. Moss will set up a space in West Palm.

## Comp (client-confirmed, internal only)
