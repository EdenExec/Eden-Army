Prepared for Joey Walker by Eden Partner Group · 12 Nov 2026 · Built from public sources only. Read on the plane; about 10 minutes.

## The 60-second version

- **Emad** is the owner's-side construction head at Related: EVP of Design and Construction, 35+ years, a registered architect and engineer by training, and the man who ran Hudson Yards. He thinks in logistics, systems, approvals and union labor across a whole campus. He is not a superintendent.
- **Bobby** is Founder, President and CEO of Promethean Builders (founded 2021). He came up as a superintendent in New York and spent 22 years with a major national GC before starting his own firm. He builds teams and cares about schedule command and the operations layer between the executive and the general super.
- **The project (public record):** Citadel's headquarters tower at 1201 Brickell Bay Drive, Miami. 54 stories, about 1,032 feet, about 1.7 million sq ft, Foster + Partners, $1B+. Related is the development partner.
- **Your angle:** speak Emad's language on logistics, vertical transportation, approvals and phasing. Speak Bobby's on schedule, teams and running the field. Bring specifics: numbers, floors, crane counts, jumps, sequences.

In the same piece he describes COVID-era slowdowns at the Building Department that made approvals harder to resolve. **How to read him**

- Owner's-side, systems thinker. Expect questions about how you coordinate trades, hoisting and vertical transportation, inspections and approvals, and how you keep a tall job moving when the city does not.
- A designer's eye. He will respect a super who reads drawings, catches coordination problems early and talks to architects and engineers without friction.
- He has built airports, transit hubs and a campus with union labor. Show you can run a heavy-MEP, high-density job inside that kind of environment.

**What he has said on the record**

- On the Google 550 Washington job (ENR): precast erection meant crews about a quarter the size of cast-in-place, and one erector could alternate steel and precast, which "gives us a lot of flexibility." He talks about crew size, sequencing and keeping trades out of each other's way.
- That project converted a four-story freight terminal into about 1.3 million sq ft over 12 stories. It was a schedule-driven, logistics-heavy job.

**How to read him**

- A former superintendent who became an executive. He judges field people fast. Lead with how you run the field, not with titles.
- He wants a layer above the general super who drives the schedule, works the APM staff and keeps the team aligned. Be ready to say how you do that, with a real example.
- Schedule is a working tool for him, not a file-cabinet document. Know your P6 and your breakout schedules.

## The project in public terms

- Citadel Global Headquarters, 1201 Brickell Bay Drive, Miami. About 54 stories and 1,032 feet, about 1.7 million sq ft (about 1.3 million office plus a rooftop hotel, dining and wellness). Foster + Partners. Reported cost: more than $1 billion. Citadel's FAA filing identified three cranes close to 1,300 feet.
- Related Companies is the development partner. Reported construction start was Q3 2025; I could not confirm current status, and no public source names the general contractor. Do not state either as fact.
- Related Companies (Stephen Ross, New York) is a different firm from Miami's Related Group (Jorge Pérez). Related and Swire's One Brickell City Centre is a separate tower. Do not mix them up in the room.


## Talking points for you

- A mega-project story with numbers: height, floors, hoists, crane plan, peak manpower, how you sequenced the core and the skin.
- Vertical transportation and hoisting: how you plan elevators for construction use, temporary hoists and the turnover to permanent cars.
- Heavy MEP: mechanical floors, medium-voltage risers, commissioning. Any data center or hospital work counts in your favor.
- Approvals and the city: how you keep inspections and sign-offs from stalling the schedule. Emad has said the Building Department can be a bottleneck.
- Union labor in a dense urban site and how you manage jurisdiction and logistics.
- Your operations-layer story: how you have run supers and APMs under you and kept one schedule.

## Questions to ask them

- **Emad:** How is the Related and Promethean team split on a joint-venture job like this? Who owns site logistics and hoisting?
- **Emad:** What went best and worst on vertical transportation at Hudson Yards, and what would you do differently on a Miami supertall?
- **Bobby:** What does a great first 90 days look like for this seat?
- **Bobby:** How do you want this role to split time between the field and the office?
- **Both:** What does success look like at topping out, and who will I report into day to day?
