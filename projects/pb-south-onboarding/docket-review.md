## 1. Sick-leave section cites New York law

**Verdict: something else. A mislabelled template, not a Florida violation. It is a real issue only if anyone works in New York.**

- **Florida requires no sick leave, and cities and counties may not require it** (Fla. Stat. 218.077(1)(d), (2), the 2013 amendment; LIKELY, from Senate analyses, statute not re-read today). So the policy is a voluntary benefit, and its terms (1 hour per about 30 worked, 40-hour cap, carryover, no payout) are lawful in Florida.
- **The NY label is wrong, and the text has drifted.** NY's law covers people who work in New York (NY Labor Law 196-b; ny.gov, read today; SETTLED). The listed uses leave out NY's own (mental illness, safe-leave services) and add school-conference and school-closure uses that read like a New Jersey template (LIKELY). The complaint line names an "Office of the Labor Commissioner", which is not NY's agency (NYS DOL; NYC DCWP).
- **Where it bites:** (a) any employee working in New York State or NYC: then NY law applies, and at 100+ employees the cap is 56 hours, not 40 (196-b; SETTLED), plus NY's 20-hour paid prenatal leave from 1 Jan 2025 (LIKELY). (b) Federal contract work could trigger EO 13706 sick leave (status in 2026 UNSURE). (c) Practical gap: PTO "may not be used for" sick reasons, so a hire who is sick past 40 hours has no paid option. Insperity should rule on all three.
