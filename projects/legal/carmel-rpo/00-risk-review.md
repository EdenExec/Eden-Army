**Verdict: a strong draft. It's clear, fair to both sides, and the money mechanics hold up. Fix the five items under "Fix before it goes" and it's ready to send.** The biggest exposure isn't in the contract text. It's in how Eden will work Carmel's California and New York roles (items 1 and 2).

## Fix before it goes

1. **Salary history: California and New York ban asking for it, and that ban covers agents like Eden.**
   - Carmel's roles are in San Francisco, Santa Monica and New York. California Labor Code §432.3 and New York Labor Law §194-a bar an employer *and its agents* from asking an applicant's pay history or relying on it. New York City's rule is even stricter.
   - Eden's usual submittal and screener carry current comp (for example, "Currently: $180k + 25% bonus").
   - On Carmel roles, ask for **pay expectations**, never current or past pay, and leave history off every submittal.
   - Add to 4.1: *"For roles in California, New York or any other jurisdiction that restricts pay-history inquiries, Eden will not ask candidates about, or report, their pay history, and will ask only about pay expectations."*
   - Grade: LIKELY. These are well-known statutes; I haven't re-read the current text today.
2. **Pay ranges in postings.** California (Labor Code §432.3(c)) and New York State and City require the pay range in job postings, and that includes postings by a third party on the employer's behalf. Job posting stays with Carmel under 4.1, but any ad Eden runs (a LinkedIn job post, an InMail template that reads like a posting) must carry Carmel's range.
   - Add to 5.3: *"Carmel provides the good-faith pay range for each Covered Role, and Eden includes it in any posting or advertisement Eden publishes."*
   - Grade: LIKELY.
3. **Section 13 is blank on liability.** The draft has no indemnity, no liability cap and no insurance clause. That matters more here because 10.1 commits Eden to sign "Carmel's standard privacy (CCPA service-provider) terms," sight unseen. Those terms often carry uncapped data-breach indemnities. Proposed language is below. Get Carmel's privacy terms and read them before signing; change "will sign" to *"will review in good faith and sign Carmel's reasonable"*.
4. **Eden's own candidate records (10.1 and 10.3).** "Does not copy it into Eden's systems" plus "delete or return Carmel data" could be read to cover the candidates Eden sourced itself, and the Submittal records Eden needs to prove a fee under 9.1's 12-month tail.
   - Add to 10.1: *"Carmel data means applicant data Carmel provides (UKG, the shared folder and employee referrals). Candidates Eden sources independently, and Eden's records of its own Submittals, remain Eden's."*
5. **Parties and addresses.**
   - Carmel's legal entity isn't named. "Carmel Partners" is a brand; use the contracting entity Brandy signs for (for example, Carmel Partners, Inc.), and define "affiliate," which 9.1 uses but never defines.
   - Eden is listed as a **Wyoming** LLC at the **Vancouver, WA** address, and 13.3 spells the address two ways. Confirm Wyoming is right (my other drafts say only "Eden Partner Group LLC"), and switch the notice address to Boise.

## Tighten (Eden's interest)

6. **Early exit (2.2).** It's unclear whether retainer invoices fall due after a termination notice.
   - Suggest: *"Retainer invoices dated before the termination takes effect remain due."* With 30 days' notice, a termination served in December still collects January.
   - Tally's angle: the whole retainer is creditable, and Carmel picks where the credit lands (6.2), including on 6% screening fees. Up to $22,500 of hires can bring in no new cash. That's by design (Option B), but know it going in. If you want cash from every placement fee, add "credit applies to no more than half of any one invoice".
7. **Screening fee and the guarantee (6.1, 8).**
   - "Internal applicant" could be read as a current Carmel employee being promoted, which invites a fight over a 6% fee. Use *"a Carmel applicant from UKG or an employee referral."*
   - Say whether the 120-day guarantee covers screening-fee hires. Suggest: placement fees only.
8. **Third-party splits (7.1).** Eden invoices Carmel and pays the agency, so Eden carries the credit risk and the guarantee for another firm's candidate. The split agreement with each agency must be pay-when-paid with Eden as client-side (as in our Vesta and Inlet paper), and must pass the replacement duty through to the agency.
9. **LinkedIn.** The agreement is clean on this: the Carmel LinkedIn page is out of scope, and nothing gives Eden Carmel's seat. Add one line to 4.1 so it stays clean: *"Eden uses its own sourcing tools and licences."*
   - On the seat itself, Rachel Bao's full reply (in the thread Brandy forwarded) goes further than the blurb. She quotes Service Terms §1.1 and then says: *"Since you aren't the Staffing Agency or BPO, you cannot reassign the license to an outside Recruiter. Staffing Agencies must purchase their own seats."*
   - So the "Eden recruiter on a Carmel email" route conflicts with LinkedIn's written answer. A Carmel-licensed seat used by Eden would put Carmel's contract at risk, and 10.1/13.1 would put that on Eden.
   - The safe route is an Eden-owned seat priced into the retainer, or Carmel assigning its seat to a Carmel employee.
10. **Confidentiality term (10.5).** Pick the number. Two years is fine, but say candidate personal data is protected for as long as it's held.
11. **No-poach (12.4).** It's fine as written: mutual, limited to the Term, and tied to the engagement. Keep it that narrow. California voids most restraints (Bus. & Prof. Code §16600), and broad company-to-company no-hire pacts draw antitrust attention. Grade: LIKELY.
12. **Late payment.** Nothing happens if Carmel pays late. Optional: *"Amounts unpaid 15 days after their due date accrue interest at 1% a month."* Section 8.2 already removes the guarantee if a fee is late, which is the stronger lever.
13. **Small fixes:**

    - 2.1 says "End Date" but the definitions use "Term … ending on"; define End Date.
    - 11.3's date fills in only once the Start Date is set.

## Section 13 language (paste-ready)

**13.1 Compliance.** Each Party complies with the employment, privacy, anti-discrimination, pay-transparency and pay-history laws that apply to its part of the hiring process. Eden presents candidates without regard to any characteristic protected by law.

**13.2 Indemnity.** Each Party will defend and indemnify the other against third-party claims to the extent they arise from the indemnifying Party's breach of this Agreement, negligence or willful misconduct.

**13.3 Limitation of liability.** Neither Party is liable for indirect, consequential or punitive damages or lost profits. Each Party's total liability under this Agreement is limited to the fees paid and payable to Eden under it in the 12 months before the claim. These limits do not apply to fees owed, a Party's indemnity obligations, breach of Section 10, or fraud or willful misconduct.

**13.4 Insurance.** Eden maintains commercial general liability insurance and professional liability insurance (or a combined policy) with limits of at least $1,000,000 per claim, and provides a certificate on request.

**13.5 Governing law and venue.** [Washington] law governs this Agreement, and the state and federal courts in [King County, Washington] have exclusive jurisdiction.

On 13.5: Carmel is in California and will probably ask for California. Eden now runs from Boise. Washington is fine if they accept it. If they push, Idaho or California with a neutral venue costs little. Your call.
