**Verdict: don't sign this as written. Send the landlord the asks below first.** The deal is fair: about $26 per square foot, utilities and common-area maintenance included, no personal guarantee, two months free. The paper is thin. It is a 15-clause form with nothing about repairs, nothing about fire or other damage, no default notice, and no way out early. Those gaps mostly cut against the tenant. None of the fixes cost the landlord much, so asking for them shouldn't spend goodwill. Exposure is about $113K over 38 months with no early exit. Because Eden Partner Group LLC is the only party on the hook, this doesn't need outside counsel unless the landlord pushes back on asks 1 to 4.

## Issues, worst first

1. **No early exit, and you're moving to Florida.** Section 1 says "Tenant shall be responsible for payment of the lease for this full term. There are no early termination provisions." If the Boise office doesn't work out, Eden owes whatever rent is left, up to about $113K. The only way out is sublease or assignment, which needs the landlord's consent (not to be unreasonably withheld). *Ask:* a one-time option to terminate after month 24 on 3 months' notice, plus a fee equal to the unamortized free rent. Fallback: let Eden assign or sublease to an affiliate or a successor without consent.
2. **Nothing on repairs.** The lease never says who fixes the roof, HVAC, plumbing, electrical or structure. Section 14 has you return the space in its original condition, and the deposit pays for "necessary repairs." *Ask:* the landlord maintains and repairs the roof, structure, building systems (including HVAC) and common areas at its own cost; the tenant handles damage it causes and the inside of the suite.
3. **Nothing on fire, flood or condemnation.** If the building is damaged and the suite can't be used, nothing stops the rent or ends the lease. *Ask:* rent stops while the suite is unusable, and either party can terminate if it isn't restored within 120 days.
4. **The use clause is too narrow for Eden.** Section 6 allows only "Staffing firm for construction companies." Eden is executive search and recruiting first, with Eden Staffing on the way. *Ask:* "general office use, including executive search, recruiting, staffing and related professional services."
5. **One-sided liability clauses.**
   - Section 15 releases the landlord from injuries or damage in the parking lot "regardless of the cause," which would include the landlord's own negligence (for example, an unplowed lot). Idaho courts generally enforce exculpatory clauses between businesses, but read them narrowly (LIKELY; not researched today). *Ask:* add "except to the extent caused by Landlord's negligence or willful misconduct."
   - Section 13 makes Eden "responsible for any stolen items and/or any damage" after a break-in whenever the front door was left unlocked. *Ask:* only where Eden was the last to leave and failed to lock up, and only for damage that failure caused.
   - Section 8 puts snow removal on the tenant. In a multi-tenant building with a shared lot, that should be the landlord's job, covered as common-area maintenance. *Ask:* the landlord handles snow and ice on the lot and shared walks.
6. **Default, notices and governing law are missing.** There is no notice and cure period before a default, no notice addresses, no quiet enjoyment, no holdover rate and no governing law. *Ask:* 10 days to cure a missed payment and 30 days for anything else after written notice; notice addresses for both sides; Idaho law; holdover at 125% month to month.
7. **Possession and the prepaid rent conflict.** Section 1 sets possession on 1 Jan 2027. Section 2 says possession comes at signing, once first month's rent, the deposit and insurance certificate are delivered. Nothing says which month the "first month's rent" pays, when rent doesn't start until March. *Ask:* it is prepaid rent for March 2027. Also: "Year 1" means the 12 months starting 1 Mar 2027. If it instead runs from 1 Jan 2027, total rent comes to about $113,120, roughly $370 more.
8. **The insurance clause is garbled.** It reads "$1.000.000.00 … for injury to one person and $2.000.000.00 for any one accident … $2.000.000.00 for damage to the property." Those are auto-policy categories. *Ask:* "commercial general liability with limits of $1,000,000 per occurrence and $2,000,000 aggregate, naming Landlord as additional insured." Have Eden's broker confirm the current policy meets it before you sign, since you'll need a certificate to get the keys. There is also no waiver of subrogation, which is standard and costs nothing.
9. **Exhibit "A" is missing.** The recital says the suite is "shown on the attached Exhibit 'A'," but the only picture in the file is the parking map, which isn't labeled as an exhibit. *Ask:* attach the floor plan as Exhibit A and the parking map as Exhibit B.
10. **Rent goes to an individual by check.** Section 3 says rent is paid to "Ying Wang" at a street address, but the landlord is Wang-Zhang, LLC. *Ask:* pay the LLC by ACH, and get the LLC's W-9 before the first payment. Eden may need to issue a 1099-MISC for rent; that's Chris's call.
11. **Smaller items:**
    - The deposit has no return deadline. Ask for 30 days.
    - Alterations need consent, and there is no signage clause. Ask for building and suite-door signage and consent for cosmetic work (paint, cabling, furniture) without approval.
    - The option should also work for an approved assignee or affiliate.
    - The late fee has no cap. Ask for a 5-day grace period, then a 5% one-time fee.
    - Typo: "$$3,131.03".
