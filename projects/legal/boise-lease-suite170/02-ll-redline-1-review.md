## 2. Early termination

**The owners' position.** Fin, 16 Nov 21:02Z: *"They have accepted all the redlines except for early termination, they are holding on this and want it to be know that you may sublease the space at any point if needed."* In the redline, s.20 is struck through and s.1 still reads *"Tenant shall be responsible for payment of the lease for this full term. There are no early termination provisions."* Section 7 is unchanged: *"Tenant shall not sublease ... or assign this Lease ... without Landlord's written consent, which consent shall not be unreasonably withheld or delayed."*

**What the owners struck** (Fin's draft, 11 Nov; Kev: "Yep good to go", 12 Nov): *"Tenant shall have the right to terminate the Lease any time after the twenty-fourth (24th) month of the Lease Term by providing Landlord with at least three (3) months' prior written notice. Upon termination, Tenant shall reimburse Landlord for the then-unamortized portion of the two (2) months of free rent ... anticipated not to exceed approximately Two Thousand Dollars ($2,000) ..."* If it is ever revived, fix three things. "Anticipated ... approximately" is not a cap, and the true figure is $2,239.87 on a straight line over 38 months. It doesn't say whether notice may be given before month 24. And there's no amortization method.

Two things soften the exposure, and one hardens it:

- **The landlord must mitigate.** If Eden stops paying and leaves, the landlord has to try to relet, and Eden owes only the shortfall. *Consolidated AG of Curry v. Rangen*, 128 Idaho 228, 912 P.2d 115 (1996), read 16 Nov 2026. SETTLED for the rule; LIKELY for how a court would apply it.
- **A sublease does not release Eden.** If the subtenant stops paying, Eden still owes the rent, and the same holds after an assignment unless the landlord signs a release. *Watkins Family v. Messenger*, 115 Idaho 386, 766 P.2d 1267 (Ct. App. 1988), read in summary only. LIKELY.
- **Losing a rent fight costs fees.** In a commercial-transaction case, the loser pays the winner's reasonable attorney's fees: Idaho Code 12-120(3), statute text read 16 Nov 2026. Applying it to a lease dispute is LIKELY.

**Fallback asks, most likely to be accepted first:**

1. **Put the owners' sublease promise in s.7 (likely: they offered it).** Add consent not "conditioned"; deemed consent after 15 business days of silence; and assignment or sublease to an affiliate or a buyer of Eden's business without consent, on notice. This was Kev's own 11 Nov ask. Optional extra: Eden is released after an assignee assumes the lease in writing. Ask once, and drop it if refused.
2. **First offer on space that opens up in the building (fairly likely: costs them nothing).** This answers Kev's real reason for an exit, outgrowing 1,403 sq ft, without the owners losing a tenant.
3. **A growth-only exit that pays them (possible, not likely).** Termination effective no earlier than 30 Jun 2029, on 6 months' notice, only if Eden needs more space and the landlord can't offer it in the building. The fee is 2 months' rent plus unamortized free rent and leasing commissions. Worked numbers: Eden avoids $25,799.68 of rent and pays $7,729.85, plus a commission share Fin would have to disclose, for a net saving of $18,069.83 before that share. The 8 months after month 30 are also gated.
