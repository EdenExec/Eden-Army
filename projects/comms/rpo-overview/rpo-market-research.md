# RPO and retained search: what the bigger firms do, and how Eden's Carmel options stack up (10 Nov 2026)

**Result:** The structures Kev drafted are standard; the mix of terms is not. A monthly retainer credited 100% against success fees is a recognized model that the RPO Association calls a "true-up": the client pays the higher of a minimum monthly fee or the placement fees. Options A and B are fairly priced for construction search. Option C is underpriced. $10,000 a month sits at the low end of what embedded recruiters cost, roughly matches what a full-time recruiter costs Eden, and has no protection if Carmel's hiring slows. Kev's other terms (100% credit, credit surviving termination, billing pause, a fee on base pay only, 30-day notice) are all more buyer-friendly than the market. That makes the offer easy to say yes to, but it gives away margin. The Talent Acquisition (TA) manager search should be its own retained search at a set fee, kept separate from the RPO term.

Grades: **SETTLED** = primary source or several independent sources agree. **LIKELY** = one credible source, or vendor pages that agree. **UNSURE** = a single vendor's marketing figure, secondhand, or inferred.

## 1. How large RPO providers price

### The models (the vocabulary Carmel's HR team will use)

| Model | How it bills | Who carries the risk | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Management fee | A fixed monthly (or hourly) fee for a dedicated team, whatever the hire count | Client | SETTLED (RPOA, HRO Today, Orion, Pin) |
| Pay for performance / cost per hire | An "open" fee per requisition and a "close" fee per hire, or a fixed fee per hire | Provider (who prices higher to cover it) | SETTLED (RPOA) |
| Hybrid (fixed + variable) | A management fee plus per-hire fees. HRO Today: the fixed part "normally falls within the 20 percent to 40 percent range of the projected monthly fees" | Shared | LIKELY (HRO Today; Pin repeats it) |
| True-up | A minimum monthly fee; the client pays open and close fees only where they exceed that minimum. "The employer pays the higher of the two." | Shared; the provider gets a floor | SETTLED (RPOA, Dec 2024) |
| Fully variable with a minimum | Per-hire fees by role category, plus a minimum volume or revenue guarantee with a year-end true-up | Shared | LIKELY (HRO Today) |
| Project RPO | A set scope and timeline (e.g. a hiring surge); often priced per transaction | Varies | SETTLED (Orion, Pin, 1840 & Co) |
| Recruiter on demand / embedded | Recruiters rented by the hour, week or month, labor only | Client | SETTLED (1840 & Co, Recruiter.com) |

**Kev's structure, in market terms:** a monthly retainer credited 100% against fees is a **true-up**: Carmel's spend is the higher of the retainer or the success fees. Use that word with Carmel; their procurement team will recognize it. (SETTLED that the model exists; LIKELY that Carmel will see it that way.)

## 2. Retained executive search norms

| Term | Market norm | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Fee | About one-third of estimated first-year **cash** compensation (base + bonus); ranges quoted 25-35% | SETTLED (Korn Ferry, Heidrick 10-Ks; Talentfoot, Paraform) |
| Uptick | If actual pay beats the estimate, bill one-third of the excess | SETTLED (Heidrick 10-K) |
| Payment | Thirds: at engagement, at slate/shortlist, at placement (some firms bill over the first ~3 months instead); owed whether or not anyone is hired | LIKELY (Talentfoot, Paraform, iRecruit) |
| Expenses | Korn Ferry adds a percentage of the fee for indirect expenses; Heidrick (2003) charged a percentage of the retainer, capped per search; one construction vendor quotes 10-15% extra for travel, assessments and checks | SETTLED for KF/Heidrick; UNSURE for the 10-15% |
| Exclusivity | Exclusive; one firm owns the search | SETTLED (AESC, Talentfoot, Paraform) |
| Guarantee | 6-12 months | LIKELY (Talentfoot; iRecruit) |
| Off-limits | Negotiated in the agreement; AESC gives no standard length | SETTLED (AESC) |

**Boutique and construction search firms differ in four ways.** They often charge a fee on base salary rather than total cash. They quote 25-33% for retained work, with 30-35% for senior roles. They use flat or partial upfront payments ($6,000-$10,000, sometimes $10,000-$20,000, credited to the final fee). They offer shorter guarantees than the big firms (iRecruit, Aug 2026). Grade: LIKELY, though the range is one vendor's.

### Option by option

| Option | Verdict | Why | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| A: $5k + 22.5% | **Fair** | 22.5% sits in the 20-25% construction contingency band; the retainer is light. Risk: Carmel asks why it should commit monthly to save 2.5 points. | LIKELY |
| B: $7.5k + 20% | **Fair, best value** | 20% matches iRecruit's published multi-role rate. iRecruit also charges $8,000/month for 2 roles, apparently not credited; Kev credits all of his. On 2 roles filled in 3 months at $150k: iRecruit ≈ $84,000, Eden B = $60,000. | LIKELY |
| C: $10k + 18%, dedicated full-time recruiter | **Cheap for Carmel at low volume, rich at high volume, thin for Eden** | $10,000/month is the bottom of the $8,000-$20,000 embedded range. Eden's own cost of a full-time recruiter (about $88,000 pay; about $126,000 loaded at a 30% benefits share) is about $10,500/month, so at low volume Eden runs the seat at or below cost. The pause clause could leave Eden paying a full-time employee with no billing. At high volume, 18% stacks on top, and Carmel will compare it to an in-house recruiter (below). | LIKELY |

### Option C vs Carmel hiring its own recruiter

| | Annual wage (BLS May 2025, HR specialists, which includes recruiters) | Loaded cost (wage ÷ 0.70) | Per month |
|---|---|---|---|
| Median | $75,940 | ~$108,000 | ~$9,000 |
| 75th percentile | $99,380 | ~$142,000 | ~$11,800 |
| 90th percentile | $128,720 | ~$184,000 | ~$15,300 |
| Paraform's fully loaded estimate | | $146,000-$200,000 | $12,200-$16,700 |

How the loaded cost is built: BLS's June 2026 data has private-industry benefits at 30.0% of total compensation, so loaded cost = wage ÷ 0.70 (about 1.43× wage). It leaves out the LinkedIn Recruiter seat, job boards, the applicant tracking system (ATS), the cost of hiring the recruiter and ramp-up time, so real cost runs higher. (Loading method LIKELY; BLS wages SETTLED.)

**What Carmel will compute:** C costs $120,000 a year at minimum, which matches an in-house recruiter at the median to 75th percentile. Above about 4-5 hires a year at $150k, C costs more than an in-house recruiter. At 10 hires it is about $270,000. Kev's answer: Carmel needs this seat now, without a hire, a ramp-up, or a long contract, and Eden brings a construction bench that an in-house recruiter does not. That argument holds for 6-12 months, not for good.

### What is missing from the draft

1. **A volume assumption.** Ask Carmel for open roles now and forecast hires for 6 months. Without that, nobody can pick between A, B and C.
2. **A requisition cap for C.** "Dedicated" needs a limit, e.g. "up to [Kev to set] open roles at once"; past that, either add a recruiter or bill at B's rate.
3. **SLAs on both sides.** Eden's side: intake within 2 business days, first slate within 10 business days, weekly report. Carmel's side: feedback within 48 hours, interview slots held. Leave out service credits unless Carmel asks.
4. **Exclusivity on the covered roles.** Carmel's other agencies either stop or go through Eden.
5. **The guarantee.** 90 days for A and B (the construction norm), and maybe 120 days for C.
6. **A term for C.** At least 3-6 months (it is a full-time seat), then 30 days' notice. Drop the billing pause for C.
7. **Credit expiry.** Market credits are non-refundable. Have unused credit expire, for example 6-12 months after the end of the term, rather than surviving indefinitely.
8. **Volume step-downs** instead of a single rate (the market multi-hire discount is 2-5 points).
9. **Expenses, tools and data.** Pre-approved travel at cost; who pays for job ads and LinkedIn; candidate data and pipeline handed over within 30 days of the end.

### Suggested adjustments (my recommendations, not market facts)

- **A:** keep it as the light option. Add exclusivity on named roles and a 90-day guarantee.
- **B:** lead with this one. It is the most defensible against any comparison.
- **C:** pick one of two fixes:
  - **C1 (keep the credit):** raise the retainer to about $12,500-$15,000/month (mid-market for embedded), credited 100%, with an 18% fee stepping down after a set number of hires. No billing pause, and a 6-month minimum.
  - **C2 (true embedded):** keep $10,000-$12,500/month **not** credited, with a lower success fee of about 10-12%. This reads like an embedded seat and stays cheaper than an in-house recruiter at volume. Eden is paid for the seat either way.

## The TA manager search inside the RPO

**Market reference:** retained firms bill about one-third of first-year cash compensation, in thirds, exclusively, with a 6-12 month guarantee (SETTLED/LIKELY above). For scale: a role at $150k base with no bonus would bill about $50,000 under one-third; at Eden's standard 25% of base it is $37,500.

**Recommendation:**
- Price it as a **separate retained search at a set fee**: 25% of Carmel's budgeted base, or a flat dollar figure agreed up front. Bill in thirds (signing, slate, start), exclusive, with a 6-month guarantee. That is below the retained norm and above contingency risk, which fits a boutique.
- **Keep it out of the RPO credit pool.** If it is paid from credits, Carmel's best-known hire looks free, and the RPO economics go quiet.
- **Decouple the RPO term from the TA hire.** Otherwise Eden earns more the slower it fills the role that ends Eden's contract; a buyer will notice. Set the RPO term on its own (for example 6 months, then month-to-month), and put a timeline on the TA search (first slate in 3-4 weeks).
- **Deal with the conflict up front.** The person Eden places will decide whether to keep Eden. Say so. Carmel's HR leader picks the finalist, and Eden includes a 30-day handover to the new TA manager in the RPO. That turns the conflict into a reason to trust Eden.
