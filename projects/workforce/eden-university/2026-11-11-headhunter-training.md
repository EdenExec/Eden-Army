## Construction · 3.5 + 3.10 (20 min): the superintendent, and who hires one

**The ladder** (titles vary by firm; ask, don't assume):

| Title | Runs | Typical scope |
|---|---|---|
| Assistant Superintendent | A trade area or phase under a super | Logs, inspections, punch, one part of the site |
| Superintendent | One job, the whole site | Schedule and 3-week lookahead, subs, safety, quality, daily reports |
| Senior Superintendent | One large or complex job, or two smaller ones | Bigger jobs, mentors assistants |
| General Superintendent | Several jobs and the supers on them | Staffing, field standards, problem jobs |
| Director or VP of Field Operations | The whole field team | Bench strength, safety record, field process |

**What a real superintendent can tell you on a screen:** peak trades and headcount on site, who built the schedule and in what tool, inspections and closeout they ran themselves, safety record and certifications (OSHA 30). A "superintendent" who was an assistant on one big job is an assistant.

**Tenure reads differently in the field.** A super who moves when a job ends is normal; a project-length stint is not job-hopping. Ask "did you finish the job?" before you count moves. (Back to 4 Nov's ice breaker.)

**3.10 · Who hires them, and what each one cares about**

| Hiring authority | Where | Cares about | Wants from us | Say | Don't say |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| VP or Director of Field Operations | Mid-size and large GCs | Bench strength, safety record, schedule performance, keeping good supers | A short list of proven builders who'll stay | "Here's what he built, the peak crew size, and his safety record" | "He's a great culture fit" with nothing behind it |
| General Manager | Regional offices, smaller GCs | Margin, the client relationship, can this person face the owner | Someone who protects the job and the name | "The owner asked for him back on the next job" | Field jargon with no business result |
| Project Executive | Where supers are hired per project | This job's schedule and the PX's own credibility | Someone who fits this job now | "He's done this building type at this size" | A generalist pitch |

**Ask on intake:** "Does the super report day to day to the PM or to a general super?" · "Ground-up or interiors? Occupied?" · "Self-perform any trades?" · "Who's the decision-maker on supers: you, the VP of Field Ops, or the PX?"

**Drill (3 min, pen):** pick one open super seat on your desk. Write who really hires it and the one line you'd lead with to that person.
