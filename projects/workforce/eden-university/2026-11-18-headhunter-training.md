## Construction · 3.4 + 3.10 (20 min): how a GC is organised, and who's at the top

**Two halves of every GC** (titles vary; ask, don't assume):

| Office side | Field side |
|---|---|
| Estimator → Senior Estimator → Chief Estimator / Director of Preconstruction | Assistant Super → Superintendent → General Superintendent |
| Project Engineer → Project Manager → Senior PM → Project Executive | Director or VP of Field Operations |
| Both report up to: **VP of Operations** (many GCs) or a **General Manager** (regional offices, smaller GCs), then the President or owner | |

Support functions that also hire: Safety (Director of Safety), VDC, Business Development, Talent Acquisition, Finance (Controller, CFO).

**How size changes it:**
- **Under about $100M:** the owner or president often is the GM; one person hires everyone. Speed matters; process is light.
- **$100M to $1B, regional:** a GM or VP of Ops runs the office; PXs and the general super hire under them.
- **National GC:** a regional office per market, each with its own GM or regional VP; Talent Acquisition sits in the middle. Know whose search it really is.

**Market sectors cut across all of it:** many GCs split by sector (healthcare, multifamily, industrial, interiors). Ask which group the role sits in.

**3.10 · VP of Operations vs General Manager**

| | VP of Operations | General Manager |
|---|---|---|
| Measured on | Delivery across all jobs: schedule, fee, margin by project, safety, staff utilisation | The whole office's P&L: backlog, win rate, margin, client relationships, people |
| Up at night | Two jobs losing money and no PM to put on the next one | Backlog for next year; losing a key client or a key PM to a competitor |
| Wants from a recruiter | Proven operators who can run a job now, and honest reads on bench depth | People who win and keep work; market intel (who's moving, who's hiring) |
| Say | "She brought her last two jobs in at or above fee" | "Two owners asked for him by name on their next projects" |
| Don't say | Vague leadership talk with no job results | Field detail with no business result |

**Ask on intake:** "Who does this role report to day to day, and who signs off on the hire?" · "Which sector group is it in?" · "Is this a backfill or growth?" (Growth means backlog; ask what's coming.)

**Drill (3 min, pen):** pick one client on your desk. Draw its chain from the open seat up to the top, and circle the person who really decides. If you can't, that's your next call's question.

## Recruiting · 2.5 (18 min): the submittal, match don't sell (deeper pass)

Kev, 16 Nov: "standardizing our submittals moving forward in favor of just matching, not selling." Module: `projects/eden-university/modules/submittal-match-not-sell.md`. Template: `employees/_shared/candidate-submittal.md`.

1. **The rule in four lines (4 min).** Must Do's from the JO, in the client's order; one bullet each, as evidence (project, size, role, years); full chronological history, with any move under 3 to 4 years explained plainly; a Must Do the candidate doesn't meet is flagged to Kev, never stretched. Kev still reviews every submittal before it goes.
2. **Selling vs matching (6 min).** Put the five "selling" lines from the module up one at a time. Each person rewrites one as a checkable fact. The test: could the client check it against the resume?
3. **Live rewrite (6 min).** Someone shares a recent submittal (theirs, by choice). The room turns each selling line into a Must Do match. Kev calls out anything that should have been flagged.
4. **This week (2 min).** Every submittal from today is on the template. Kev reviews the first one from each person.
